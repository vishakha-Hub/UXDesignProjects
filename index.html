Case Study 01

Mountain View
Library Redesign

The Friends of the Mountain View Library website served as the digital gateway to a beloved community institution — but usability issues, cluttered navigation, and an outdated GUI were preventing users from getting what they needed.

GIT 542: Usability / UX
Amber Draina
Fall 2022
Figma, Google Docs
Heuristic Eval, User Interviews, Usability Testing
High-fidelity prototype

The Problem

Users across all age groups — from a 14-year-old student to a 65-year-old retiree — struggled to find basic information on the site. Navigation was disorganized and repetitive, the GUI was visually dated, book listings were absent, and selling used books was confusing. The site needed a complete UX overhaul grounded in research.

01

Heuristic Evaluation

Applied Jakob Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics, grading issues on Molich & Jeffrie's severity scale. Identified disorganized navigation and poor GUI design as critical problems.

02

User Research

Online surveys + in-person interviews with 3 users (ages 26–65). Identified overwhelming content, missing book information, and confusing navigation as top pain points.

03

Persona Creation

Created 3 personas — Noah (high school student), Emma (software engineer & mother), and Charlotte (retired teacher) — to humanise the data and guide design decisions.

04

User Stories

Developed user stories for each persona with acceptance criteria, covering book discovery, online purchasing, events, membership, and volunteering needs.

05

Usability Testing

Created test materials, conducted sessions, and analysed data. Focused on 4 critical issues: navigation, book listings, used book selling flow, and overall GUI quality.

06

Wireframes → Prototype

Developed site plan, low-fidelity wireframes, and high-fidelity Figma prototype covering Home, Events, Volunteer, and Application Form pages.

!
Navigation Overload

Top navigation was disorganised with repetitive links — resolved through restructured IA and a clear 5-section nav.

!
Missing Book Listings

Users couldn't find available books — resolved by creating a dedicated Book Shop section with search and categories.

!
Confusing Sell Flow

Selling used books was unclear — resolved with a dedicated volunteer/sell page with a clear step-by-step process.

✓
Events Calendar Added

Users wanted event visibility — designed a dedicated Events & News page with a calendar and event cards.

"UX isn't just about fixing problems — it's about creating experiences that evolve with users."

This project taught me that rigorous research is the foundation of confident design. Every decision — from the site map to the volunteer form — was grounded in something a real user told me they needed. Working through multiple research phases deepened my ability to synthesize data into actionable design improvements and communicate those decisions clearly to stakeholders.

Case Study 02

Wedding Bells
Planning App

Despite an abundance of wedding planning apps, users still felt overwhelmed, stressed, and unsupported — particularly around budget management and family coordination. Wedding Bells was designed to solve exactly that.

TWC 544: User Experience
Dr. Lynne Cooke
Summer 2022
Figma
Perfect score · A grade
iOS Mobile App

The Problem

5 user interviews revealed that budget constraints and family involvement were the two biggest stressors in wedding planning. Existing apps lacked collaborative features and overwhelmed users with complexity. The opportunity was to build something that felt personal, simple, and genuinely collaborative — not just a digital checklist.

01

Strategy

Defined user needs through interviews: budget management, personalised task tracking, family collaboration, and a simple interface. Conducted competitive analysis of existing wedding apps.

02

Scope

Defined functional requirements (budget management, task lists, vendor/venue management, RSVP tracking, registry integration) and content requirements across all app sections.

03

Structure

Mapped interaction design across 3 core interfaces (Home, Wedding Planning, To-Do List) and created a full information architecture from Home Page through all sub-features.

04

Skeleton

Created hand-drawn low-fidelity sketches followed by digital wireframes for all main screens: home, budget manager, to-do list, guest management, and family sharing.

05

Surface

Produced high-fidelity Figma prototype using a consistent colour system, typography, and navigation patterns. Designed for iOS with a bottom navigation bar and clear visual hierarchy.

06

Outcome

Received a perfect score. Dr. Cooke noted the app idea may fill a genuine market need and praised the quality of both low and high-fidelity wireframes.

"Designing across cultures taught me that wedding planning is deeply personal — and design must honor that."

This was my first project in the UX graduate program. Creating "Wedding Bells" challenged me to apply the full Jesse James Garrett 5-planes model to a real consumer product from scratch. It gave me insight into cultural nuances in design and the importance of involving family dynamics in UX thinking — not just individual user journeys.

Case Study 03

Desktop Lamp
Redesign

Traditional desk lamps have remained functionally unchanged for decades. A team of five redesigned the desk lamp from the ground up using Human Systems Engineering methodology — earning a perfect score of 50/50.

HSE 521: Human Systems Engineering
Dr. Jaimie McNabb
5 members
50/50 · Course grade 94.5% (A)
Survey, 3D Prototyping
Physical 3D prototype

The Problem

User research with 12 survey respondents and 5 observed users identified five core frustrations: difficulty turning the lamp on/off in the dark, inability to adjust brightness easily, limited design flexibility, lack of multi-purpose features (USB, clock, wireless charging), and absent safety indicators. The brief was clear — redesign the lamp to solve all five.

01

Survey & Observation

12-response online survey + 5 observed user tasks. 66.7% wanted better articulation and design aesthetics; 41.7% prioritised USB ports. Toggle switch ranked most preferred (41.7%).

02

Heuristic Evaluation

Individual evaluations combined into a team review. 5 findings identified and ranked by severity, each with specific design recommendations for the new prototype.

03

Journey Mapping & Personas

Created 3 personas (Jessica — eco-conscious designer; Amber — busy student; Brian — mechanical engineer) and a shopping journey map across Consider → Explore → Compare → Test → Negotiate stages.

04

Low-Fi Sketching

Hand-drawn sketches exploring articulating neck, dimming bar, USB ports, temperature controls, timer, and night light functionality before committing to a direction.

05

3D Prototype

Built a high-fidelity 3D prototype with articulating neck, pivoting shade, smart on/off, app-controlled dimmability, USB port, wireless charging dock, and operation indicator light.

06

Usability Testing

Tasks: turn on, adjust brightness, charge device. Critical findings: need for voice control signifier, charging indicator, and dimmability signifier — iterated into final design recommendations.

✓
Smart Switch Added

Voice and app-controlled switch solved the dark-activation problem entirely.

✓
App-Controlled Brightness

Dimmability integrated via app with a physical dimming bar for quick manual adjustment.

✓
Multi-function Base

USB port + wireless charging dock + operation indicator light added to base.

→
Signifiers Needed

Usability testing revealed missing visual signifiers for voice control and charging status — identified as next iteration focus.

"Designing physical products taught me that empathy-driven design transcends screens."

This was my first physical product design project, and it fundamentally expanded my understanding of UX beyond digital interfaces. Using 3D prototyping tools was a new skill, and working in a team of five with diverse perspectives taught me the value of collaborative synthesis. The project reinforced that user frustrations — however small — represent genuine opportunities for innovation.
